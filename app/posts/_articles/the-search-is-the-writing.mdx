## Two descriptions of one thing

The account of creativity I hear most often is that it means connecting fields that look unrelated. Teng Bao's [Creativity Is a Search Problem](https://www.tengbao.me/blog/creativity-is-a-search-problem/) gives a different one: every possible work already sits somewhere in an enormous space, and creating is "using heuristics to iteratively reduce a large search space" until what remains is good enough.

These describe the same activity. Most heuristics tell you to look near where you already are. A connection between distant fields is a heuristic that sends you somewhere far away, to a region nobody working on your topic has a reason to visit.

## Generating was always the cheap half

The [monkeys at their typewriters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_monkey_theorem) eventually produce Hamlet, and Borges's [Library of Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Library_of_Babel) holds every possible book. Neither has ever been mistaken for an author.

There is also a version that actually happened. In 2020 Damien Riehl and Noah Rubin wrote a program that enumerated every 8-note, 12-beat melody, about 68 billion of them, and [released them into the public domain](http://allthemusic.info/). Their point was about copyright law. The side effect is a hard drive containing every pop hook of that shape, including the ones nobody has written yet, and it has not put a single songwriter out of work.

Language models did the same thing to prose. I can have twenty competent openings in a minute, and knowing which one to keep did not get any cheaper. So the question I care about is where one person's heuristics come from, because that is the part the model does not supply.

## Pre-writing is where the space shrinks

swyx's [Mise en Place Writing](https://swyx.io/writing-mise-en-place) borrows the kitchen practice of preparing every ingredient before you cook. Pre-writing is a constant, low-intensity habit of capturing ideas and attaching links to them as they turn up; drafting is a separate sitting that goes fast because everything is already on the counter.

In his version the drafting is still a block of hours. In mine it has mostly disappeared: a full draft of this post existed within a quarter of an hour of the dump.

What I had wrong was what pre-writing produces. I thought of my notes as ingredients that would end up in the dish, but almost none of the dump's prose survived. What it contains is a list of decisions: which two framings are secretly one, which examples count as evidence, which other post this one reinforces, and that my own workflow is the case study. Each of those rules out an enormous number of possible posts. The prep, in my kitchen, is less about chopping onions than about deciding what is for dinner.

## Ripe

The phrase in the dump I can explain least is "when the time is ripe."

Early on, a new note could belong to any of a dozen posts. The notes are ripe when the next thing I save only fits one, and I can say in a sentence what they have in common. For this post the sentence was that Teng Bao and swyx were describing the two halves of what I do every time I write.

That is a description after the fact, and I do not really have a system for it.

## What the search frame leaves out

Search assumes the space and the target hold still. [Margaret Boden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Boden) separates exploring a conceptual space from transforming it, and the works people remember longest tend to be the ones that changed what counted as good. I think this objection is correct and I do not have an answer to it. A blog post that connects two other blog posts is exploration, and I am comfortable with that.

## Whose search

Claude produced most of the sentences in this post, so "did AI write this?" has an easy answer and not a very informative one. A better test is to hold the model fixed and change the pre-writing. Ask it cold for an essay on creativity and search and you get a competent essay that is not this one. Hand a stranger my dump and I expect they would land close to it.
